- Project layout: raw / interim / processed data split so every step can be rerun from the untouched downloads.

## 2026-10-01
- surveys_land.py: all Bundesland sections are read from the single overview page and concatenated with an added `Bundesland` column, in the same layout as surveys_bund. The combined cells "Institut (Datum)" and "Befragte / Zeitraum" are split into separate columns, `CDU` and `CSU` are merged into `CDU/CSU`, and the client (`Auftraggeber`) is dropped. `FW` and `BSW` are split out of `Sonstige` into their own columns; all other remaining parties (e.g. PIRATEN) are summed into a single `Sonstige` value, which is left empty if any part is unknown. ## 2026-10-06
- btw_wahlkreise.py: official election results by constituency are extracted from bundeswahlleiterin.de for all federal elections from 2013 onward. Party results are mapped into standard groups (CDU/CSU, SPD, Grüne, FDP, Linke, AfD, FW, BSW), while non-group parties and remaining valid votes are aggregated into Sonstige. Non-constituency rows (e.g. state summaries) are excluded to retain strictly constituency-level granularity (299 constituencies per election). Directly elected candidate parties are parsed from the winning column or determined as fallback via the highest first-vote (Erststimmen) count.

## 2026-10-07
- btw_gender_age.py: Results of the representative election statistics: Second votes by gender and age group. Only elections from 2013 onward are kept. `CDU` and `CSU` are summed into `CDU/CSU`, and columns are renamed to the project's standard party names (`Grüne`, `Linke`). Values are shares of second votes in percent. `Geschlecht` is kept as in the source (`m`, `w`, `Summe`; from 2021 `m|d|o`, because the source counts diverse persons and persons without gender entry under men), and the overlapping age groups (`Summe`, `>=60`, `60-69`, `>=70`) are all kept, so rows must not be summed across age groups.
- regierungszufriedenheit.py: government satisfaction ("Wie zufrieden sind Sie mit der Arbeit der Bundesregierung?") is taken from the ARD DeutschlandTrend PDF reports, from the February 2013 edition onward. Because the PDF layout changed several times since 2013 (portrait/landscape, rotated pages, one or two slides per page), values are located by position: the number directly above each category label is taken as that category's value. PDFs without the question (state polls, special editions) or with only aggregated values (e.g. April 2018) are skipped, and duplicate reports of the same survey are stored once, keyed by the end date of the survey period (`Datum`). For 2013–2015 PDFs where the chart is a raster image, values are reconstructed as "reference edition + changes" from the text deltas, with bar heights measured on the rendered page as fallback and cross-check.

PDFs without the chart (2017) are skipped. Values are exit-poll percentages as printed in the PDF, mostly rounded to whole numbers, so a row's parties do not always sum to exactly 100.
 
## 2026-10-09

- ltw_results.py: official state election results are taken from the Bundeswahlleiterin PDF "Ergebnisse früherer Landtagswahlen"; the PDF is only downloaded if not already present. Because every page shows two election tables side by side, each page is split into a left and a right half by word coordinates, and the election date is read from the table headline ("Wahl am DD.MM.YYYY"). Only elections from 2013-01-01 onward are kept (44 elections, all 16 states), and the cut is applied before the column selection, so changing the window means rerunning the scraper with another `START_DATE`. Values are vote shares in percent as printed in the PDF, i.e. the vote that decides the seat distribution (second votes in two-vote states, all votes cast in Hamburg and Bremen). Party name variants are mapped to the project's standard columns (`CSU` and `CDU` to `CDU/CSU`, `DIE LINKE.` to `Linke`, `GRÜNE/B 90` to `Grüne`, `BVB/FREIE WÄHLER` to `FW`, `AfD Niedersachsen` to `AfD`), and `SSW` gets its own column; any other party with at least 5 % in one of the kept elections gets its own column before `Sonstige` (currently only `BIW`, Bremen 2023). A party that did not stand is left empty rather than 0, and `Sonstige` is 100 minus the sum of all listed columns. The Berlin 2023 repeat election is stored under the original date 2021-09-26, as printed in the PDF.
- Impact measure: instead of a hand-assigned rating per event, derive it from tagesschau.de coverage: salience = share of archive teasers in a window that match the event's keywords; tone = mean sentiment of those teasers (german-sentiment-bert on the teaser text). Rationale: measured, reproducible, independent of the outcome variable.
- tagesschau_archiv.py: scrape from 2013-01 (AfD founding). Items are de-duplicated on article URL. Raw HTML is kept per page so the parser can be re-run offline.
- The archive thins out going back (Jan 2013 ~100 items/month vs Oct 2025 ~1,200): counts must be normalised by the month's total before comparing years; daily resolution is only meaningful for recent years.
- Sentiment will be computed on `shorttext` (teaser), not on headlines alone. Weather and Marktbericht items should be flagged/excluded before sentiment aggregation.

## 2026-10-12
- controls_monthly.py: the two monthly control series for Q1 come from Eurostat instead of the Bundesagentur für Arbeit and BAMF, because Eurostat offers both as one consistent monthly series since 2013 through a free API without a key, while the German sources would need manual Excel/PDF downloads per year. Unemployment is the ILO-harmonised rate from the Labour Force Survey (`une_rt_m`, geo=DE, seasonally adjusted, total), which runs about 1.5 to 2 points below the BA "Arbeitslosenquote" quoted in German media because unemployed and labour force are defined differently; seasonal adjustment is used because polls are compared across months and the raw rate has strong winter seasonality. Asylum applications are first-time applicants (`migr_asyappctzm`, applicant=FRST, i.e. Erstanträge), not TOTAL, because subsequent applications are repeat claims by people already in Germany and do not measure new arrivals. Eurostat rounds counts to multiples of 5. Caveat: the series counts formal applications filed with the BAMF, which lagged the arrivals of autumn 2015 by months, so it peaks in 2016-08 (92,115) rather than in autumn 2015 (about 58,000 per month). Months not yet published are left NaN.
- btw_strukturdaten.py: the official constituency structural data ("Strukturdaten für die Wahlkreise") are taken from the Bundeswahlleiterin CSV of each federal election from 2013 onward; files are only downloaded if not already present. The files are semicolon-separated with a preamble, CP 1252 (2013, 2017) or UTF-8 with BOM (2021, 2025), and use decimal commas; "–" (2013) and "." (2017) are the source's missing markers and become NaN. Only constituency rows (Nr 1–299) are kept; the state and federal summary rows are dropped because btw_results.csv already covers these aggregates. Because the variable set and names differ by year, a core set is mapped to common column names via a per-year dictionary (`COLUMN_MAP`) in the script; variables a year does not publish are left NaN (2013: disposable income and GDP). The 2013 foreigner share is not published and is derived from the published total and German population counts, the only computed column. Vehicle density is kept as two columns, because 2013/2017 count all motor vehicles and 2021/2025 passenger cars only. The Abitur share includes Fachhochschulreife from 2017 on but not in 2013, and the reference dates of unemployment and SGB II differ by year; both are kept in one column and noted in the docstring. SGB II recipients stay per 1000 inhabitants as in the source. The source footnotes are kept in `Fussnote`, because for cities with several constituencies (Berlin, Hamburg, München, Köln) most variables are city-wide values repeated per constituency, which matters for a regression. Constituency names are kept as in the source; against btw_wahlkreise.csv all 299 names per year match once dash variants and spacing are normalised (2013 kerg writes "Berlin-Treptow - Köpenick", Strukturdaten "Berlin-Treptow-Köpenick"), so the merge must normalise names or use the constituency number.

- taschenhirn.py, event dates: the page only has a year column, but most entries name the day in the text. The day is parsed from the text with a fixed set of patterns ("15. April", "14./15. Juli", "8. bis 25. Februar", "6.2.2023", "19.12.", and month-only "Anfang/Mitte/Ende/Ab/Im Oktober", "Dezember 2019"). The first token whose year equals the row's year is used; tokens with another explicit year are skipped because they are context ("Wahlsieg vom 8. November 2016" in a 2017 row). Month-only tokens give the first of the month with `datum_praezision` = monat; entries without any token keep `datum` empty with precision jahr, nothing is looked up or guessed. `datum_roh` keeps the matched fragment so every date can be checked against the text. Result for the 89 entries from 2013: 61 day, 11 month, 17 year only.
- Event sources, common layout: all event scrapers (taschenhirn, Wikipedia year pages, geopol, English crisis list) write the same columns so they can be concatenated into the `events` table: `quelle`, `quelle_url` (page plus section anchor or entry link), `quelle_permalink` (Wikipedia oldid where available), `abgerufen_am`, `jahr`, `abschnitt`, `unterabschnitt`, `verschachtelt`, `datum`, `datum_ende`, `datum_praezision` (tag | monat | jahr | fehlt), `datum_roh` (the matched fragment), `text_roh` (as printed), `text` (cleaned), `stichworte` and `stichworte_urls` (link titles and URLs inside the entry, later the matching keywords). Every value is parsed from the page; nothing is looked up, inferred or completed. Month-only dates get the first of the month, year-only dates YYYY-01-01 (a convention for sorting, visible through `datum_praezision`), no token leaves `datum` empty with `fehlt`.
- geopol_timeline.py: the date line of each card is matched as a whole against four patterns ("Month D, YYYY" -> tag, "Month YYYY" -> monat, "YYYY" -> jahr, "YYYY – YYYY" -> jahr with datum_ende). Only entries with year >= 2013 are kept (41 of 124). The era heading goes to `abschnitt`, the category label to `unterabschnitt`, `text` is title + summary, the entry's own article link is `quelle_url` and the single `stichwort` is the title. The page has no revision permalink. Its `data-year` attribute is only used as a printed cross-check.
- wikipedia_crises_en.py: year sections >= 2013 are selected by heading; the page has no 2016 section in revision 1368984378. Each bullet becomes a row; nested bullets become their own rows with `verschachtelt` = 1 and the umbrella text(s) in `unterabschnitt` joined by " > ". The same umbrella entry listed under several years ("Middle Eastern crisis (2023–present)" under 2023 to 2026) is kept once per section as printed, so duplicates must be handled in the merge. Dates are the most precise token in the bullet's own text; a day range without year ("May 19–28") takes the year from the section heading; "2023–present" leaves `datum_ende` empty. `jahr` follows the token and can differ from the section ("2022 – 2024 German economic crisis" under 2024 has jahr 2022). Because a leading year in a title counts as the date token, `text` can lose it or end in a stub; `text_roh` and `stichworte` keep the full wording. Plain-text bullets without links have empty `stichworte` (12 rows).
- wikipedia_jahr.py: the German Wikipedia year articles 2013–2026 are the main event source (`events_wikipedia_jahr.csv`). Only the "Ereignisse" section is read and only its subsections on politics, economy and disasters are kept, including their sub-subsections (Terroranschläge, Naturkatastrophen, D-A-CH); Sport, Kultur und Gesellschaft, Religion, Wissenschaft und Technik, Astronomie, Wetter und Klima, Wissenschaftspreise and Gedenktage are skipped because they are not plausible drivers of party preference. Because the section titles change between years, every occurring variant is listed in `KEEP_SECTIONS` ("Wirtschaft und Infrastruktur" 2017–18, "Wetter und Katastrophen" 2021–26, "Schwere Unglücksfälle" as own section 2022–26, "Anschläge" 2021); the mixed 2020 section "Gesellschaft, Kultur, Wirtschaft", "Soziales" (2013) and "Bereits feststehende Ereignisse" (2024–25) are not kept, and 2019, 2020, 2022–2024 have no economy section at all. Dates are parsed only from the start of the item's own text with fixed regexes (day tokens "15. April", "14./15. Juli", "7.–9. Mai", "23. April und 7. Mai"; month tokens "August", "Ab August", "August/September", "Dezember 2019"): `datum` is the first day of a range and the 1st of the month for month-only tokens, `datum_ende` the second date; the year is taken from the token if printed, otherwise from the page. Items whose text does not start with such a token ("Sommer 2022:", "Siehe auch: …", "vermutlich 18. Juni:", "Ab August zunehmend:") get an empty `datum` and `datum_praezision` = "fehlt" instead of a guessed date; nested items without own token inherit the parent's date (`verschachtelt` = 1). Wikipedia's hidden padding spans make "01. Januar" and "000Juli" appear in `text_roh` and `datum_roh`; the regexes allow the leading zeros and the tokens are kept verbatim. Keywords (`stichworte`, `stichworte_urls`) are not written by hand but taken from the Wikipedia article links inside the item (title attribute, in order, deduplicated; footnote, external and red links excluded), so they are reproducible from the source. Known quirk left unpatched: in 2013/Wirtschaft the five items under "1. Januar:" are an indented sibling list in the HTML, not a nested list, and are therefore "fehlt".
- tagesschau_umfrage.py: infratest dimap exit-poll charts are read from the alt texts of the chart images in the tagesschau.de election archive for the Bundestag elections 2013, 2017, 2021 and 2025, for the pages umfrage-alter (age), umfrage-job (occupation), umfrage-werwas (gender, education, workers, first-time voters) and umfrage-afd (AfD-specific; in 2013 that URL serves the general "Aktuelle Themen" charts, including one on the AfD). There are no umfrage-geschlecht or umfrage-bildung pages. The first version only produced 2025 rows because the older pages use different alt-text formats, not because of HTTP errors: three formats are parsed (2025 pipe format with previous value or signed change in brackets; 2013–2021 comma format without previous values, so `value_prev` is empty before 2025; a nested "group: category" variant, flattened to category "Partei: Gruppe"). In 2021 the numbers sit on one sub-page per chart, which are fetched too. Only images in the page's own chart folder are kept, because every page also embeds teaser charts of other pages, which had inflated and duplicated the first CSV (2025 umfrage-alter 173 -> 115 rows). Where 2025 prints a signed change instead of the previous value, `value_prev` is derived as value minus change; the source's placeholder "+10003" for BSW (no 2021 value) is left empty. Encoding: the server sends no charset in the HTTP header, so `requests` decoded the UTF-8 pages as ISO-8859-1 ("GrÃ¼nen"); the bytes are now decoded as UTF-8 and the earlier corrupted raw files were re-downloaded. Three charts whose alt text has no numbers at the source (one each in 2013, 2017, 2021 umfrage-afd) are skipped; repeated category labels (2017 AfD by gender: Männer 13, Frauen 8, Männer 25, Frauen 16, sub-groups not named in the source) and the differing source spelling ("Infratest-dimap" vs "Infratest dimap") are kept as printed. Cross-check: the 2025 charts' "previous" values equal the 2021 charts' values for every AfD age group.
- Rejected source, cabinet law tables: the German Wikipedia cabinet articles (Kabinett Merkel II to Merz) were tried as a source for dated domestic-policy events (laws passed). Only the Kabinett Scholz article has a legislation section (49 rows, in-force dates only, 2022–2025); the Merkel and Merz articles and the Bundestag legislative-period articles (18., 19. Deutscher Bundestag) have none. Too thin and too late (in-force dates lag the debate by months), so the scraper was dropped. Domestic policy debates are instead to be detected from the tagesschau archive itself (terms whose weekly share of teasers jumps), see the upcoming salience work.
- Scope of events: AfD-internal happenings (party splits, leadership changes, Verfassungsschutz classifications) are deliberately not collected. The research question asks about national and international events acting on the AfD, not about the party's own history.
- Sentiment (tone) demoted to a secondary measure: the March 2026 prototype (german-sentiment-bert on 1,101 teasers) labelled 79 % neutral, and its extremes were dominated by irrelevant sections (asparagus season, weather, astronomy) or wrong in direction ("Erde erwärmt sich schneller" +0.79). News tone is near-tautologically negative for crises and the model was trained on reviews and tweets. Tone will, if used at all, be computed only within teasers matched to an event or theme, never over all teasers, and reported with this caveat. An LLM-based labelling of teasers was considered and rejected by the team: results would not be reproducible from the source.
- Section filter for all teaser-based measures: only the sections inland, ausland and wirtschaft are used (85 % of the archive). Other sections (wissen, kultur, sport, wetter, multimedia, podcasts) are not politically relevant and were the main source of noise in the sentiment prototype. About 800 rows carry a URL or slug instead of a section name (a parsing edge case in tagesschau_archiv.py, to be checked) and drop out with this filter.
- Salience by word bursts instead of keyword lists (`src/preprocessing/burst.py`, prototype on four test weeks): teasers are split into tokens (words of at least 4 letters, not in a fixed German function-word list, cut to 6 characters so inflections and compounds collapse); each token's baseline is its share of all teasers 2013–2026, smoothed with 20 pseudo-documents so that words occurring a handful of times in 13 years cannot produce absurd ratios; a token's burst in a week is its share of that week's teasers divided by the baseline, listed only if it occurs in at least 3 teasers of the week. A teaser's importance is the mean burst of its 3 burstiest tokens; because the raw scale depends on how rare the week's key words are (Credit Suisse 68 vs Corona 33), it is only compared within a week, and two normalised columns are used across weeks: importance_rel (share of the week's maximum) and importance_rank (percentile within the week). Validated by eye on 2020-03-16 (Corona, Ausgangssperren), 2015-09-07 (Juncker refugee quota speech), 2023-03-13 (Credit Suisse, SVB) and 2026-03-09 (Iran war, oil reserves, Landtagswahl BW): in all four the top tokens and top teasers are the week's main story and the bottom teasers are one-off items scoring about 1. Weeks before 2020 have 11–60 teasers, so bursts there rest on few documents and the smoothing matters most. This replaces matching Wikipedia link titles as keywords (tested in event_salience.py: titles like "Anschlag in Hanau 2020" match no teaser, while single place names work but need hand selection); the keyword-based script is kept for comparison until the burst-based event salience is implemented. Bursting tokens per week also serve as data-driven detection of domestic policy debates (the source gap noted above).

## 2026-10-13
- Data model, Wahlkreise: the processed table carries Erststimmen and Zweitstimmen shares per party and the party of the directly elected candidate, not only second votes, because direct mandates (AfD in the East) are part of Q2.
- Data model, area naming: every table uses `level` (Bund | Land) and `bundesland` (empty for Bund); the value "Bundesgebiet" and the column `gebiet` are dropped so the tables join without renaming.
- Polls by week: a poll is assigned to the ISO week of the midpoint of its fieldwork period (`Zeitraum`), not of its publication date. Measured on surveys_bund.csv: INSA, Forsa, Infratest dimap, Forschungsgruppe Wahlen, YouGov and GMS field over 3 to 7 days and publish about one day later; Allensbach fields about 14 days (up to 20) and publishes a week later; Verian 7 days, up to 21. The 17 polls whose Zeitraum is a single date use that date. Each poll keeps `fieldwork_days`. The weekly series uses every poll, with house effects removed per institute and party so that the changing institute mix between weeks does not move the series. The event study uses only polls with fieldwork of at most 7 days, because a poll fielded over two weeks cannot show a reaction to an event inside that window; this drops about 5 % of polls, nearly all Allensbach. Spreading long polls across the weeks they overlap was considered and rejected as harder to explain for little gain.
- Event salience, keyword rule: the Wikipedia link titles (`stichworte`) are not usable as match terms. Checked over all 1,269 day-dated events of events_wikipedia_jahr.csv: titles like "Anschlag in Hanau 2020" never appear in a teaser, only 503 events got any match, and where there was a match it was mostly a generic word (deutschland, israel, euro). Replaced by a data-driven rule in build_event_salience.py: the match terms of an event are the word stems of its own `text` (words of at least 4 letters, not in the stopword list, cut to 6 characters, as in burst.py) that occur in at least 3 teasers of the event week or the week after and whose share there is at least 10 times their smoothed share over the whole archive. Generic stems never burst and drop out by themselves; nothing is hand-picked. The terms actually used, with their burst factor, are stored per event in `terms` so every score can be traced.
- Event salience, weekly table and score: salience of a week = matching teasers / all political teasers (sections inland, ausland, wirtschaft) of that ISO week, for weeks -4 .. +12; a week without teasers is NaN, not 0. One score per event: `peak_share` = the larger of week 0 and week +1 (both are checked because an event late in the week is covered in the next ISO week; 105 of 296 usable events peak at +1), minus `baseline_share` = mean salience of weeks -4 .. -1, so that an escalation of a topic already in the news is not credited for attention that was already there. Events with fewer than 3 matching teasers in the peak week keep their value and get the flag `duenn`; events without any bursting stem get `keine_terme` and NaN. Checked on three such events (Istanbul attack 2016, Sindschar 2014, Wanka 2013): the archive really has only 1 to 2 teasers on them, so the flag marks thin coverage, not a parsing error. Sum over the window, raw counts and a composite index with sentiment were considered and rejected as harder to explain. Deliberately kept simple; the weak points go into the report: teasers only, three sections only, archive about 10x thinner in 2013 than 2025, the Wikipedia sentence may not use the words the news used, an escalation of an ongoing topic does not burst.
- Event salience, status: the two files event_salience_weekly.csv and event_salience_summary.csv are written to data/interim/ as candidates for the processed layer (tables `event_salience` and the event summary in docs/data_model.md). Result on 2026-10-13: 1,356 events, 296 usable, 972 without terms, 87 without a day date, 1 thin. The tone columns of the data model stay empty because sentiment is scored for one month only. The earlier event_salience.py (prototype with stichworte matching, `--terms` to try alternatives) is kept for exploration.

## 2026-10-20
- Event sources reduced to the German Wikipedia year pages (`events_wikipedia_jahr.csv`). The three secondary event sources were compared against it and are not used further: taschenhirn (89 rows from 2013, 61 with a day date; 43 of these 61 also appear in the Wikipedia year page of the same day with the same key words, the remainder are mostly sport and culture items such as the 2014 World Cup final or the Olympic Games, or sit under a neighbouring date; the CSV also still has the old column layout with `ereignis` instead of `text` and no permalink, section or keywords), geopol.uk (41 rows, 1 with a day date, 38 month-only) and the English crisis list (76 rows, 3 with a day date, 54 year-only, 16 without date). The three scrapers and their CSVs stay in the repo for transparency and are marked "evaluated, not used" in docs/sources.md.
- Data model split into two parts: the deliverable 1 data model describes the collected datasets as they are in `data/interim/` (the dataset the two-page report quantifies); the processed layer with polls_weekly, events, event_salience etc. is kept as the prospective model for deliverables 2 and 3. Salience, sentiment, the house-effect adjustment and the international/national classification are processing steps and are not part of deliverable 1.
- Known deviation left as is for deliverable 1: `surveys_bund.csv` and `surveys_land.csv` carry `Datum` as DD.MM.YYYY and `Befragte` with a thousands separator ("1.051"), as printed on wahlrecht.de, which contradicts the ISO-date / plain-number rule for interim files. Converting is a one-line step in the processing layer; the interim files are left as scraped so the raw values stay checkable against the source.
- Events, two sources with different roles: the Wikipedia year pages are the event list (one row = one dated event with text); the tagesschau archive is a news stream (one row = one article) and is not an event list as collected. The two do not overlap fully in either direction, and the gaps are handled asymmetrically. Wikipedia events without archive coverage are kept with salience about zero, because an event that did not register in German news is a comparison case, not missing data. Archive topics without a Wikipedia event (domestic debates such as Heizungsgesetz or Bürgergeld) are not turned into dated events, because that would need hand-entry; instead they are summarised per ISO week from bursting word stems (`burst.py`) as a prospective table `topics_weekly`. A burst topic has no start date and no sentence, only a week and a few words, so Wikipedia events are the clean treatments and burst topics a second, coarser set that is interpreted more cautiously. Both derivations are processing steps after deliverable 1; the report states the plan.
